What Aetna pays therapists, by license and billing code

Georgia and Florida · Aetna's published rate file, Nov 5, 2026

By Kurt Niemi, who builds Pablo.

How we figured this out, and the full method

Aetna publishes every rate it has agreed to in a huge public file. The first look made no sense: almost every practice had three different rates listed for the same session code, and the file gave no hint which one applied. An easy explanation was "plan tiers" or "office versus telehealth", and we nearly stopped there.

Kurt Niemi, who builds Pablo, kept pushing. Office versus non-office only gives two rates, not three, and the file showed the three prices were identical in every other way. So we looked at the numbers themselves. In every practice and every code, the three rates sat at exactly 75%, 85% and 100% of each other, to the cent.

That split is familiar: Medicare pays clinical social workers, marriage and family therapists and counselors 75% of the physician rate, and nurse practitioners 85%. Then we found contracts in the same Aetna file that do put the license code next to the rate (AJ/HO for social workers and master's-level clinicians, TD for nurse level, AF/AH for physicians and psychologists). Those labelled rates sit at the same 75%, 85% and 100%. Aetna's own provider newsletter calls 85% its "standard policy" for nurse practitioners, and an independent analysis of Aetna's data describes the same ladder.

So the three rates are a license ladder: master's level at 75% of the top rate, nurse practitioners at 85%, doctoral at 100%. Every practice's contract lists all three, and the one you are paid depends on your license. Once we read it that way, the numbers lined up, and they are what this page shows.

The full method, and what it can't tell you

  • Source: Aetna Life Insurance Company's in-network rate file "pl-sk" (2026-11-05), used by 457 employer plans and covering Open Access Managed Choice and Choice POS II. Other Aetna files were not checked yet.
  • State and license come from the federal NPI registry (November 2026): practice-location state and primary taxonomy. Florida LMHCs are counted with LPCs.
  • Region: practice-location ZIP from the NPI registry; a group counts in the region where most of its clinicians practice.
  • The license rates: most practices list three rates per code at 75%, 85% and 100% of the top one, without saying which is which. A few contracts in the same file label their rates with Aetna's license modifiers (AJ/HO master's level, TD nurse, AF/AH physician or psychologist), and those sit at exactly the same 75/85/100. Each clinician here gets the rate for their own license. Outside support: Aetna's own March 2022 provider update calls 85% of the physician rate its "standard policy for mid-level practitioners" (nurse practitioners, physician assistants, nurse midwives, clinical nurse specialists), and Serif Health's September 2026 analysis of Aetna's data describes the same "standard rate ladder": nurse practitioners at 85% and master's level at 75% of the PhD rate. No public Aetna document found states the 75% for master's level in its own words.
  • Florida solo practices whose rates follow the 75/85/100 pattern: 99%; Georgia: 95%. The rest (mostly older round-dollar schedules in Georgia) don't say which rate is which license and are left out.
  • Standard rate: the most common rate for that license and code in the state (in Florida, in the region). "Exactly the standard rate" means paid that rate, to the cent.
  • Low and high: the lowest and highest rate at least 11 practices are paid (or the 10% and 90% marks if those are further out), so no single practice's rate is shown.
  • A few contracts (about 1%) also split each rate into office and non-office (facility, telehealth) prices. Only office or all-places prices are used here.
  • Solo means a tax ID with exactly one clinician anywhere in the file. Everything else is a group. Platforms: the rates are what Aetna pays the platform's tax ID (Florida, 90837, read with the same 75/85/100 pattern). What a platform passes on to its clinicians is not in the file.
  • Rates under $20 were dropped as placeholders. Some listed contracts are dormant: a clinician can be in the file and rarely bill Aetna.
  • Medians and ranges only. No individual clinician's rate is shown, and we leave out any row based on fewer than 11 practices, so no number can point to one clinician.

Start here: what does Aetna pay someone like you?

  1. Find your license in What each license is paid (LCSW, LPC / LMHC, LMFT, nurse practitioner, psychologist, psychiatrist).
  2. Look at the dashed line first. That is Aetna's standard rate for your license: what a practice on the standard contract is paid, and what a new contract usually starts at. Florida has two, one for each region (see which Florida region you are in). Georgia has none, so its charts have no dashed line.
  3. Then look at the dot and the bar. The dot is the median. The median is the rate in the middle when practices are lined up from lowest to highest. Many practices are paid exactly the same rate, so the median is often the standard rate itself. The bar shows where most practices are paid (the middle half). In Florida most solo practices are paid exactly their region's standard rate, so there the bar is often a thin mark on the dashed line. If the bar reaches well past the dashed line, many practices like yours are paid more than the standard rate.
  4. In Florida, check How often Aetna pays exactly its standard rate for how many solo practices Aetna pays exactly that, and what it pays the rest.
  5. Use the big table at the bottom for other codes (45-minute sessions, intakes, family sessions).

These are the rates Aetna has agreed to for its main national network, published because federal law requires it. They show the standard rates, not what your own contract pays. If you're paid less than the standard rate for your license, ask Aetna's provider line why you aren't on it.

Aetna lists three rates for each practice and pays the one that matches your license: master's level (LCSW, LPC / LMHC, LMFT), nurse practitioner, or doctoral (psychologist, psychiatrist). Florida has two standard sets, one for each region. Georgia has no standard set; it is below.

Which Florida region are you in? It goes by where you practice. North and central Florida is ZIP codes starting 320 to 329, 344 and 347: Jacksonville, St Augustine, Daytona, Tallahassee, the Panhandle and Pensacola, Gainesville, Orlando and Winter Park, Kissimmee, Ocala, the Space Coast and Vero. Everywhere else is south Florida and Tampa Bay: Miami-Dade, Broward, Palm Beach, Tampa, St Petersburg, Sarasota, Fort Myers, Naples, Lakeland and Port St Lucie. Practices on the two sides have the same mix of licenses and contracts of the same ages; the difference is location. A group counts in the region where most of its clinicians practice.

Georgia: no single standard rate

Georgia has no standard rate the way Florida does: no one rate covers most solo practices. For a 60-minute session (90837), a separate $65 schedule, Georgia's counterpart to Florida's $60, covers about a quarter of master's-level solo practices (26 of 105). Most of the rest are paid between about $99 and $112; the median for all of them is $102.20. In this file, rates in metro Atlanta are about the same as in the rest of the state.

Some Florida contracts pay less. A separate Aetna fee schedule pays master's-level clinicians $60 for a 45-minute session and the same $60 for a 60-minute one ($70 for psychologists). It is the same schedule in every employer plan in this file, not one plan's. It belongs mostly to agencies and group practices, and to long-established independent practices: all but five of the 55 got their NPI in 2005-07, when NPIs were first issued, and only one is newer than 2013. Clinicians who joined those agencies recently are on it too, but almost no independent practice that started recently is.

Organizations on it with 11 or more Florida clinicians include Aspire Health Partners (Orlando, 166 clinicians), Henderson Behavioral Health (Broward, 147), Alpha Therapy (Arcadia and Bradenton, 74), Cove Behavioral Health (Tampa, 55), Park Place Behavioral Health Care (Kissimmee, 28), Samaritan Counseling Services of the Gulf Coast (Sarasota, 17), Weston Psychological Associates (16), Child and Family Psychologists (Plantation, 16), Presbyterian Counseling Center (Daytona Beach, 14), Adapt Behavioral Services (Maitland, 13) and Guidance Clinic of the Middle Keys (Key West, 12). Aspire and Park Place are in north and central Florida, where the standard rate for independents is $102.94.

If you are independent and were offered $60, ask Aetna which fee schedule and which plans the offer covers, and what the standard rate is for your region.
